Detroit to Fort Myers Beach

Our Story

Two retirees, a concession business, a beach trailer — and a lot of Coney dogs.

From Detroit to the Beach

Scott and Trina Legas spent years running a concession business back in Detroit, the kind of work that's busy, hands-on, and never really lets you slow down. After they retired, they didn't exactly retire — they just changed coastlines.

As longtime snowbirds, the Legases had visited Fort Myers Beach for years before they ever sold a single hot dog there. They knew the beach, the regulars, the rhythm of the place. So when the idea of starting a Coney Island-style restaurant in Florida came up, it didn't feel like a stretch — it felt like the next natural step.

"We Started Out for Fun"

"We started out for fun," Trina said. "We weren't sure we were going to do it full-time because it's a lot of work." That honesty is exactly who they are — no big corporate plan, no franchise playbook. Just two people who loved the beach, loved feeding people, and figured they'd give it a shot.

It turned into a lot more than fun. Detroit Dawgs became a real stop on Estero Blvd, and the "we'll see how it goes" attitude turned into a full-time labor of love.

Rebuilding After Hurricane Ian

Like so many businesses on Fort Myers Beach, Detroit Dawgs faced the aftermath of Hurricane Ian. The storm tested the whole island — homes, businesses, and the community that holds it all together. Scott and Trina rebuilt, just like their neighbors did, and came back ready to keep serving the beach they love.

Today, Detroit Dawgs has a permanent spot on Estero Blvd, right across from the Lani Kai Island Resort — proof that the comeback was worth it.

A Little Bit of Michigan on the Beach

Fort Myers Beach sees a huge wave of Michigan snowbirds every winter — people who grew up on Coney dogs and know the difference the second they see Koegel's on a menu. For them, Detroit Dawgs isn't just lunch. It's a taste of home.
